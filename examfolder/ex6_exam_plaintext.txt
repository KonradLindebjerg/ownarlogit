The rules of the game
Your task is to write a program that turns your robot into a rally racing machine. The robot should be
able to complete a race track as fast as possible without bumping into obstacles using no more than 15
minutes to complete the track. The race track consists of 4 known unique landmarks positioned in a
square (see drawing in figure 1). Each landmark contains ArUco codes on the sides (with the same ID
on each side), and the ArUco ID uniquely identifies the landmark (see table 1 for a definition of each
landmark). Inside the race track there will be randomly positioned obstacles in the form of cardboard
boxes with ArUco codes on the sides. The obstacle ArUco code IDs are unique and larger than 4,
ID > 4.
Your robot will start in the vicinity of landmark L1 and you may assume that it faces the landmark.
You may not assume anything about the distance to or position around the landmark. Your robot must
visit the landmarks in the order L1, L2, L3, L4 and finally back to LI. Your robot must only visit a
landmark once, the exception to this rule being the final L1-visit. A landmark is visited, if the robot (or
parts of it) is within 40cm distance to the landmark (measured to the closest box side). Your robot has
completed the race track when it returns to landmark LI and the time it took is recorded. The racing
team completing the track using the shortest amount of time, including penalty minutes, is declared
the winner of the race.
The obstacles will be placed at random inside the race track square. On race day the referees will place
the obstacles on the race track. Therefore, you cannot assume anything about the location, orientation,
and number of these obstacles, except that they will be fully or partially inside the race track square.
You may also assume that obstacles have ArUco $ID>4$ and have unique IDs.
Your team has in total 20 minutes to setup and race, and when the 20 minutes are over, your robot will
be removed from the race track. As soon as your robot moves by itself the clock starts counting down
on your 15 minutes race time. It is therefore important that your racing team is ready to go when it is
your time to race.
If your robot fails to complete the race track within 15 minutes, the robots current position is recorded.
If no race team completes the track, the team that has visited most landmarks and comes closest to the
final landmark L1 is declared the winner of the race. A verdict will be reached by the referees based
on how many landmarks have been visited by each team.
The race schedule will be announced just before the race start. All teams are expected to be present
during all races. The race day will end with the announcement of the champions of DIKU RoboRally
2026.
Penalties: Penalties will be given by tough, but fair, referees and will be added to the time it takes
your robot to complete the race track:
• If your robot bump into a landmark or obstacle your racing team will receive a penalty of 3
minutes for each collision.
• If your robot misses a landmark (the robot does not come within 40cm distance to the landmark),
your racing team will receive a penalty of 14 minutes for each missed landmark.
• If your robot visits the landmarks in a different order than L1, L2, L3, L4, and finally again
L1 or revisits a landmark, your racing team will receive a penalty of 14 minutes each time this
occurs.
Suggestions on how to solve the exercise
You need to come up with a strategy for completing the race track as fast as possible while avoiding
getting penalty minutes. You may use any part of your solutions to the previous exercises as you see
fit. You can reuse your solution to the self localization exercise and extend it to include four land-
marks and use this to localize your robot on the race track. To avoid obstacles you can use either or a
combination of your obstacle avoidance solution or your local path planning solution (this could allow
you to see obstacle from a distance and plan ahead). However, you are warned that a very advanced
solution may not pay off and the KISS (Keep It Simple Stupid) principle may still be a very valid
design principle.
